# Google Play Store Apps — Where to Build Next

**Tier A diagnostic + predictive review of 9,659 Play Store listings**, following the McKinsey
communication standard (Pyramid Principle, MECE, SCR storyline, answer-first delivery).

> **Governing thought.** Enter on monetization structure rather than crowding, band expected reach
> with a model that must never be used to pick winners, and drop app rating from the greenlight
> scorecard entirely — it cannot be forecast, and the field itself is censored.

## The business question

> Which Play Store categories and listing configurations offer a defensible entry for a new app —
> and how much of an app's reach can actually be anticipated from its listing before launch?

Framed as a brief to a **Head of Mobile Product** at a mid-size app studio, ahead of a next-build
greenlight. The decision is threefold: which category to enter, whether to launch free or paid, and
how much weight to put on a pre-launch reach forecast.

## Headline findings

| | |
|---|---|
| **15.1%** | of apps carry no rating — median **1 review**, **100 installs** |
| **0.073** | HHI — the catalog is *unconcentrated*; crowding is not the constraint |
| **0.503** | reach macro-F1 vs a **0.132** majority baseline (3.8×) |
| **0.074** | rating R² against a **0.15** bar fixed *before* any model ran → **rejected** |

### 1. The catalog's quality metric is structurally censored

Google withholds the star rating until an app accumulates reviews. Missingness is therefore
**generated by the outcome**: 100% of zero-review apps are unrated, decaying monotonically to 0%
across all 21 install brackets (Cochran–Armitage z = −58.6).

The 1,463 unrated apps have a median of **1 review and 100 installs**; the rated ones, **3,017
reviews and 100,000 installs**. Dropping them — the standard move — deletes **91.8% of the niche
tier**. Every "average rating by category" figure computed from this dataset is a **survivor
statistic**.

**The control that makes this defensible.** The obvious objection is that small apps simply carry
thinner metadata. If so, other optional fields would thin out the same way. They do the opposite:
rating missingness correlates **−0.55** with install tier while `"Varies with device"` correlates
**+0.32** (Size) and **+0.30** (Android version). The censoring is specific to the field.

### 2. Crowding is not the entry constraint — monetization structure is

At **HHI 0.073** the catalog is unconcentrated by any competition-policy standard (`FAMILY` leads at
19.6%; top 5 of 33 categories hold 46.3%). What separates categories is whether they sustain paid
distribution at all: **PERSONALIZATION 21.5%** and **MEDICAL 20.7%** against a 7.8% catalog average,
while **HOUSE_AND_HOME, BEAUTY and COMICS sustain none** — Wilson CIs clear of the average, Holm-corrected.

### 3. Reach travels with maintenance and payload, not with quality

Days-since-update (ρ = **−0.33**) and app size (ρ = **+0.31**) are the strongest listing associations.
Category explains just **2.6%** of rating variance. All **associations, not causes** — popular apps can
afford maintenance, so causality plausibly runs backwards.

### 4. Reach is forecastable pre-launch; rating is not

A tuned model reaches **macro-F1 0.503** (95% CI [0.489, 0.518]) on decade-scale install tiers —
but finds only **26% of blockbusters**, mistaking 294 of 437 for the tier below. **That caps it to
budget *banding*, never greenlight *selection*.** Eight tuned model families missed the
pre-registered rating bar, peaking at R² 0.074.

## Three corrections to the standard analysis of this dataset

This dataset is among the most-analysed on Kaggle. Three findings contradict how it is usually handled:

| # | The common approach | What this analysis found |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Rating` is *13.6% missing* — drop or impute | It is **structurally censored**, not missing. Imputing fabricates the long tail; dropping produces survivor statistics. |
| 2 | Deduplicate on `App`, keep max reviews | That deletes **85 genuine cross-listings** — `FAMILY` is a Play Store *overlay*, so an app legitimately holds two categories. **Two grains** are maintained instead. |
| 3 | Feed `Reviews` to an install model | `Reviews` reproduces **91.3%** of install variance on its own. R² goes 0.42 → **0.94** when added — that is the outcome fed back in, not model skill. |

A fourth is about specification rather than data: at the raw **21-bracket** resolution the target is
effectively unlearnable (**macro-F1 0.098** — the model names the modal bracket and little else).
Collapsed to **4 decade-tiers** the same features reach 0.503. *The formulation is the finding*, and
accuracy is what hides it — which is why macro-F1 is the primary metric throughout.

## Method notes

**Statistical discipline.** At n = 9,659 a Cohen's *d* of 0.06 clears p < 0.05, so **effect sizes lead
every claim and p-values live only in tables**. Wilson intervals on every proportion (the normal
approximation fails at the 0% and 100% cells this data contains). Benjamini–Hochberg FDR within each
test family; Holm within the 33-category paid-share family. Every "no meaningful effect" claim ships
with its **minimum detectable effect at 80% power**, so a null is a *powered* claim. Non-parametric
companions accompany parametric tests (ratings are skewed −1.75) and agree with them.

**Leakage control.** `Reviews` and derivatives, all install aliases, `Rating`, and `Rating_Observed`
are banned from pre-launch models. `Rating_Observed` is the subtle one — the censoring result makes it
a near-deterministic function of install tier, i.e. a **disguised label**. The ban is asserted
programmatically against the *fitted* transformer's output feature names, not merely intended.

**Pre-registration.** The rating acceptance rule (CV R² ≥ 0.15) was written into the notebook as a
markdown cell **before any rating model ran**, with the threshold set deliberately low so a rejection
could not be blamed on a demanding bar. Eight families were built and tuned regardless, so the verdict
belongs to a serious attempt.

**Fairness.** A protected-subgroup audit is **impossible** — the data describes apps, not people — and
that impossibility is reported as a main-narrative limitation rather than omitted. The two runnable
audits (per-category error rates, per-tier recall) were executed.

## Limitations

- **Survivorship, and it is not fixable.** Only apps live at scrape time are present; delisted and
  failed apps are absent, so every "successful category" statistic partly reflects which failures were
  removed.
- **`Installs` is a bucket floor, never a count** — 21 ordinal brackets. Every install figure is a
  lower bound and is labelled as one; no mean of bucket labels is ever computed.
- **Vintage is August 2018** (max `Last Updated`), not "early 2019" as the source documentation states
  — that is the Kaggle publication date.
- **No causal claim is available**, and none is made.
- **No revenue, retention, developer identity, region, or review text** — monetization findings stop
  at list price.
