Creature-balance review · Tier A

Budget power by tier, template only the types that have a signature, and skip the type checker

Pokémon base stats, Generations 1–6: 721 species, six stats each, 18 types. How much a creature's type should constrain its power and its stat profile, and whether a model can check new designs for off-type stats.

SituationThe 721 species of Pokémon Generations 1–6 each carry six base stats and one or two of 18 types. Designers and players talk about types as having typical stats (Steel is bulky, Electric is fast), and this dataset is a standard exercise in predicting type from stats.

ComplicationNobody had measured how much type actually constrains a stat line, and the file makes it easy to get wrong. Its 800 rows include 79 forms that copy a species, evolution siblings share a stat shape, and the legendary flag mixes two definitions. Validated with a random split, a type-from-stats model looks 34% accurate, and a team could build a design check on that number.

Type is a tilt, not a template. A creature's power is set by a few fixed tier rules, not by its type, and type shapes the stat profile of only 10 of 18 types. Validated honestly, stats name the type 21% of the time, against 14.6% for always guessing Water. The design team should budget power by tier, template stat shape only for the signature types, and not build a stats-to-type checker.

  1. The fileThe 800 rows are 721 species plus 48 Mega Evolutions and 31 alternate forms that reuse a species' number and type. The legendary flag misses 8 species the games class as legendary or mythical. A species' nearest match in stat shape is within two dex numbers 26% of the time, against 0.6% by chance: evolution families break independence, so every model must be validated by family.
  2. PowerTotal follows tier rules. 53 of 54 legendary species sit at 580 or more, the 7 pseudo-legendaries sit at exactly 600, and 47 of 48 Megas add exactly 100. Legendary status explains 26% of the variation in Total, type 7%, and type's link does not survive correction once legendary status is held fixed. There is no generational climb (ρ = 0.06).
  3. ShapeDivide each stat by Total and type explains at most 28% of any stat's share (Sp. Atk). Tested on 360 independent families, 10 of 17 testable types carry a signature: Steel and Rock in Defense (+8 and +9 points of every 100 against other families), Fighting in Attack, Electric in Speed, Normal in HP, and others. Water, Grass, Bug, Ice, Poison, Dragon and Ghost are shaped like the average species.
  4. PredictionKept apart by family, a random forest names the type 21% of the time (± 1.8% across seeds) against 14.6% for always guessing Water, and 20.5% on an unseen generation. No pair of stats beats 19%. Only 7 types are recognisable (AUC ≥ 0.75), so a checker would be wrong about four times in five.

Recommendation — Lead creature-balance designer

  1. Adopt a power-budget ladder from the corpus. Ordinary creatures stay below 580 (the ordinary roster tops out at 567, Archeops; Slaking's 670 is the one exception). Give each roster one 600-point pseudo-legendary line. Place legendaries at fixed steps from 580 to 720. Specify any temporary power-up form as “+100 Total, same primary type”, so balance work goes into where the points go. Owner: balance lead, before creature sheets are drafted.
  2. Write stat-shape templates for the 10 signature types only. Use the per-type tilts in Part III (for example Steel: about +8 points of Defense per 100, −4 of Speed) as defaults a designer can override. Give Water, Grass, Bug, Ice, Poison, Dragon and Ghost no template: the corpus shows none, and inventing one would narrow those types for no reason. Owner: creature designers, in the template document.
  3. Do not build a stats-to-type classifier as a design check. At 21% honest accuracy it would flag mostly noise. If a check is wanted, make it a soft warning for the 7 recognisable types (Steel, Fighting, Normal, Fairy, Psychic, Electric and Ground), such as “this Steel design has less Defense than 9 in 10 Steel creatures”, that a designer can dismiss. Owner: tools team, this sprint (redirect the classifier work).
  4. Validate every model on this roster by evolution family or by generation. A random split reported 34% for a model whose honest score is 21%. Owner: data team, as a standing rule.
  5. Add the missing columns before the next balance review. Evolution family and stage would explain much of the 69% of Total that tier, type and generation leave open. Abilities, moves and a usage tier are needed to say anything about strength in play. Fix the legendary flag at the source. Owner: data team, before the next review.
So what
The key exhibit. The same model scores 34% under a random split and 21% when evolution families are kept together. The difference is memorisation: with a sibling in training, the model copies the family's type. Predicting a whole unseen generation gives 20.5%, close to the honest 21.2%. A label-shuffled model never exceeds 13.0% (40 runs), so the signal is real, but it beats “always Water” by only 7 points.

The 800 rows are 721 species plus 79 forms, and evolution siblings share a stat shape 46× more often than chance

So what
Every Mega and alternate form keeps its species' number and primary type, so a model trained with forms in both halves of a split is shown the answer. Evolution families do the same more quietly: siblings are numbered consecutively and built on the same proportions (26% of species have their nearest stat-shape neighbour within two dex numbers; chance gives 0.6%). Analyses here use the 721 species, and models are validated with families kept together.

Power comes in tiers: 53 of 54 legendary species sit at 580 or more, and the 7 pseudo-legendaries at exactly 600

So what
Legendary and mythical species use a handful of fixed Totals (580, 600, 660, 670, 680, 720); the only one below 580 is Phione. A Total of 580 or more identifies them with 53 hits, 1 miss and 8 false alarms: the 7 pseudo-legendaries (Dragonite, Tyranitar, Salamence, Metagross, Garchomp, Hydreigon and Goodra) and Slaking. Against the file's raw flag the same rule raises 15 false alarms, seven of which are the flag's own errors.

Mega Evolution is a fixed budget: 47 of 48 Megas add exactly 100 points

So what
The exception is Mega Alakazam (+90). Every Mega keeps its species' primary type. Alternate forms follow no single rule: 20 of 31 re-spread the same Total (Rotom, Deoxys, the Therian formes) and the rest add 40–100. A power-up form is a +100 budget; the design work is where the points go.

No steady power creep: the median ordinary species sits between 400 and 450 in every generation

So what
Generation and Total correlate at ρ = 0.06 [0.01, 0.12] among ordinary species; generation accounts for well under 1% of the variation, and neither species-level test survives correction. Taking each family's strongest member as its final stage, generations differ modestly (ε² = 0.08), as a dip in Generations 2–3, not a climb. Generation 4's bump fits its many late evolutions for older lines. The budget ladder needs no per-generation adjustment.

Raw stats all rise together; once size is removed, the trade-offs appear

So what
Every pair of raw stats correlates positively (mean ρ = 0.39): bigger creatures are bigger everywhere. Divide by Total and the designers' trade-offs show: Attack × Sp. Atk ρ = -0.36, Attack × Sp. Def ρ = -0.56, Defense × Sp. Atk ρ = -0.41. Comparing types on raw stats mostly compares their mix of evolution stages and legendaries, so Part III and the models use shares.

Some types have a clear stat signature; about half are shaped like the average species

So what
Steel and Rock put about +8 and +6 more points of every 100 into Defense than the average species, mostly out of Speed. Tested family by family and corrected for 119 comparisons, 10 types keep a signature (Steel, Rock, Fighting, Ground, Fairy, Psychic, Electric, Normal, Fire and Dark). Water, Grass, Bug, Ice, Poison, Dragon and Ghost do not; Ghost and Dragon, which the data review expected to be distinctive, are among them. Flying has only 3 species as a primary type and cannot be judged.

Type explains at most 28% of the variation in any stat share, and 7% of Total

So what
Even Sp. Atk, the share type shapes most, keeps about three-quarters of its variation for other reasons: role, stage, second type. For Total, type explains 7% against 26% for legendary status alone. Type is a tilt, not a template.

No pair of stats identifies type: the best, Attack × Sp. Atk, scores 19.3%, 5 points above always guessing Water

So what
The dataset's premise, that type cannot be read from Attack and Defense, holds, but not because those two are a poor choice: Attack × Defense ranks 4 of 15 (16.9%). It holds because no pair works. The fifteen pairs span 11.2% to 19.3% against 14.6% for always guessing Water, and the top pairs are within seed noise of each other. All six shares together reach 21.2%. Type is not written into any two stats.

Only 7 types are recognisable from their stats, all with a signature; 7 cannot be told from a coin flip

So what
Steel, Fighting, Normal, Fairy, Psychic, Electric and Ground clear an AUC of 0.75: a model can rank their members above other creatures. Dark, Bug, Water, Poison, Flying, Ice and Ghost have intervals that reach 0.5. A soft warning is defensible for the first group and would flag only noise for the second.

No natural archetypes either, with one small exception. Clustering the stat shares gives a silhouette of 0.18–0.20 for every k from 2 to 8 (0.5 or more indicates separate groups), a few hundredths above data with no structure (0.14–0.15). Roles such as wall, sweeper and special attacker are useful labels, but the data shows them as cuts through a continuum. The one sharp group is 21 HP specialists (Chansey, Blissey and Wobbuffet, …) that put 35% of their Total into HP.

Appendix · Method and limits

Hypotheses that did not survive

“Steel, Rock, Ghost and Dragon have the clearest signatures” (data review). Half right. Steel and Rock have the two strongest; Ghost and Dragon have none that survives a family-level test, while Fighting, Ground, Fairy, Psychic, Electric, Normal, Fire and Dark do.

“One row per species; ~721 rows, no generation or legendary columns.” Wrong on each count, and the legendary column that does exist mixes two definitions.

“A stratified split is enough.” It is not: evolution siblings inflate accuracy by 13 points.

“Natural archetypes exist.” Not as separate clusters (Part IV).

“Dual types carry a stat premium.” Their median Total is higher (452 against 405), but within families the dual-typed member is the later stage in 83% of 52 cases. It is an evolution-stage effect, not a design rule.

What this analysis does not claim

Nothing about strength in play. Base stats are inputs to the damage formula; moves, abilities, levels and items decide battles, and none is in the file. “Power” here means the Total budget only.

No causation. Stats do not cause type or the reverse; the signatures describe how the designers of Generations 1–6 allocated points, and are offered as defaults, not laws.

Gen 1–6 values as recorded. Later games changed some base stats and added generations. The family proxy (consecutive species sharing a primary type) misses evolutions added in later generations, so the honest score is, if anything, slightly optimistic.

Cleaning. Nothing was imputed or dropped. Glued form names were split (94 rows), forms flagged, a corrected legendary_canon flag added beside the raw one, and 18 species with an extreme stat (Blissey, Shuckle, …) flagged and kept as design choices. The log is data/processed/_cleaning_log.csv.

Statistics. Rank-based tests (Mann–Whitney, Kruskal–Wallis, Wilcoxon) with rank-biserial r, ε² and η² as effect sizes. Type signatures are tested on 360 family means, and intervals resample families, because a family repeats one design. 119 tests were corrected together (Benjamini–Hochberg); 39 survive. A signature needs q < 0.05 and |r| ≥ 0.30.

Models. Random forest (300 trees) and multinomial logistic regression on the six stat shares; Total never beside the six stats. Five seeds, family-grouped 5-fold CV, leave-one-generation-out, and a 40-run label-permutation null (p = 0.024). The model card is models/type_classifier_card.json. Fairness auditing does not apply: no people are affected.

Reproducibility. Six numbered notebooks run in order with fixed seeds, and a restart-and-run-all from the raw CSV is verified. They import no local modules: chart styling and every helper are defined inline. Every number in this report is read from reports/_key_figures.json, written by notebooks 01–05.
